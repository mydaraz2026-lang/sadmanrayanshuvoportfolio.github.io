Market Teardown · Bangladesh Fintech

The wallet is solved.
Distribution isn't.

Bangladesh's mobile financial services sector moved Tk 17.37 lakh crore (~$158B) in 2024 — up 28% year on year, and still climbing in 2026. On paper it looks like a solved digital economy. Underneath, it's still a cash-handling business wearing an app.

Nagad's Q1 2026 transaction value hit a record Tk 111,355 crore, up 21% YoY.

Distribution moat

1.83 million agents nationwide (Dec 2024), up from 1.53 million in 2022. The agent network — not the app — is still what makes cash usable as digital money in rural Bangladesh.

Reach

~237 million registered accounts against a population of ~170M — more accounts than people, but only 37.6% (~89M) are active. Dormancy, not registration, is the real inclusion gap.

03 · What the volume hides

This isn't a payments economy. It's a cash-handling economy with an app on top.

MFS accounted for over 83% of all transaction volume in the financial system in a recent month — but only 7.28% of transaction value. Most of that volume is cash-in and cash-out, not commerce.

05 · What's coming

Bangladesh Bank is about to commoditize the wallet layer

Bangladesh Bank began building the Interoperable Instant Payment System (IIPS) in 2025 — a single rail connecting banks, MFS providers, and fintechs in real time. Today, moving money between bKash and Nagad, or between a wallet and a bank, is friction by design. That friction is what protects the duopoly's fee structure.

Once wallets are interoperable, switching costs collapse. The two incumbents stop competing on "does your money move" and start competing on merchant acceptance, agent trust, and service — the same commercial battlefield as any other retail distribution business.

06 · Where this is going

The next fight isn't in the app. It's in the agent network and the merchant till.

01

Interoperability erodes the wallet moat

When IIPS lands, "which wallet do you have" stops being the question. Whoever wins post-IIPS wins on distribution and service quality, not on network lock-in.

02

Merchant payments are the underbuilt layer

Only a sliver of MFS value is genuine digital commerce today. The provider that builds real merchant acceptance — not just P2P and cash-out — owns the next growth curve.

03

This is a channel-management problem, not a UX problem

1.83M agents is a dealer network with a fintech license. Winning it looks like BSRM's dealer-territory discipline, not another app redesign — and that's a commercial skill set, not a product one.
